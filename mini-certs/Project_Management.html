Project Management Certificate

Prerequisites: Software Development Concepts Certificate

Final Goal: Students will practice planning and steering a software project themselves — not just describing what a project manager does, but doing the actual planning work: gathering requirements, scoping, estimating, and reflecting afterward.

Learning Targets

  • Determine project objectives and identify stakeholders and their roles
  • Collaborate with users to gather requirements and structure a project's scope
  • Estimate resources, time, cost, and priorities, and document the personnel/technical skills a project needs
  • Research technologies and produce a technical design document and test plan
  • Document lessons learned after a project wraps up

Proof

Each checkbox represents a proof of understanding. Students must demonstrate mastery by actually producing the planning artifact, not just describing it.

Project Vision & Stakeholders

  • Vision Statement – Write a one-paragraph vision statement for a project that states its objective and why it matters
  • Stakeholder Map – Identify every stakeholder in a project (users, sponsor, dev team, etc.) and explain each one's role and what they need from the project

Requirements & Scope

  • Requirements Gathering – Interview or survey real users (classmates count) about a problem, and turn their answers into written requirements
  • Scope Document – Define and structure a project's scope of work — what's in, what's explicitly out, and why
  • Prioritized Feature List – Produce a prioritized feature list (e.g., must-have/should-have/nice-to-have) for a project based on the gathered requirements
  • Other: ________________________________

Planning & Estimation

  • Resource & Cost Estimate – Estimate the time and cost needed for a project's scope, and show the reasoning behind the numbers
  • Priority Tradeoffs – Given more requested features than time allows, decide and justify what gets cut or deferred
  • Skills & Personnel Plan – Identify and document the technical skills, personnel, and technology a project would need to complete
  • Other: ________________________________

Technical Planning & Quality

  • Technology Research – Research and justify at least 2 technology choices for a project against its actual requirements and constraints
  • Technical Design Document – Write a technical design document for a small feature, describing how it will be built
  • Testing Techniques – Identify at least 3 testing techniques appropriate to a project and explain what each one catches
  • Test Plan – Create and implement a test plan for a project, including what will be tested and how success is measured
  • Other: ________________________________

Retrospective

  • Lessons Learned – After completing a project, document at least 3 concrete lessons learned — what would you do differently, and why
  • Other: ________________________________
✅ Overall Mastery
Defines a project's vision and stakeholders, gathers real requirements and scopes a project from them, estimates resources/time/personnel realistically, produces the technical planning artifacts (design document, test plan) that guide the actual build, and reflects on what worked once the project is done.

Success Indicators

Students are ready for advanced project work when they can:

  • Turn a vague idea into a written scope that a team could actually start building from
  • Defend a priority decision (what got cut, what didn't) with real reasoning, not just gut feeling
  • Write a test plan before writing the code it will test
  • Honestly document what went wrong on their own project, not just what went right

This Project Management Certificate treats planning as a skill to practice, not a role to describe — every artifact here is something a student produces and defends, not a job title they memorize.
