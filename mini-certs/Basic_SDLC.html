Software Development Concepts Certificate

Prerequisites: None

Final Goal: Students will understand why software projects are organized into a life cycle, compare the major methodologies used to run that life cycle, and understand how a project's major roles fit into its phases and what each one actually produces.

Learning Targets

  • Define the Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) and explain why methodology choice is an organizational decision, not a matter of one approach being inherently best
  • Compare the Agile and Waterfall approaches to running that life cycle
  • Identify the major roles on a software project team, which SDLC phase(s) each works in most, and the main artifacts each one produces

Proof

Each checkbox represents a proof of understanding. Students must demonstrate mastery through explanation, diagramming, or application.

Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC)

  • SDLC Definition – Define SDLC (Software Development Life Cycle) in your own words
  • Why Methodology Choice Varies – Explain why an organization would pick a specific SDLC methodology

Agile Methodology

  • Agile Definition – Define Agile in your own words and explain its iterative nature
  • Value: Individuals & Interactions – Explain the Agile value "individuals and interactions over processes and tools"
  • Value: Working Software – Explain the Agile value "working software over comprehensive documentation"
  • Value: Customer Collaboration – Explain the Agile value "customer collaboration over contract negotiation"
  • Value: Responding to Change – Explain the Agile value "responding to change over following a plan"

Waterfall Methodology

  • Waterfall Definition – Define the Waterfall model and explain its linear, sequential nature
  • Sequential Process Diagram – Diagram the Waterfall flow: Requirements → Design → Implementation → Verification → Maintenance
  • Waterfall Case Study (Composite) – Analyze a real-world project and explain when Waterfall would be appropriate vs. when it would be problematic

Choosing a Methodology

  • Agile vs. Waterfall Scenario (Composite) – Analyze a software project scenario and explain how Agile (applying the 4 values above) would approach it differently than Waterfall

✅ Overall Mastery
Defines SDLC and explains that methodology choice is an organizational decision, compares Agile and Waterfall (including each Agile value) and picks the right one for a scenario, and — for each major project role — identifies which phase it works in most and lists the artifacts that role is actually responsible for.

Success Indicators

Students are ready to build on SDLC fundamentals when they can:

  • Explain, for an unfamiliar project scenario, which methodology fits best and why
  • Look at an artifact (a vision statement, an ER diagram, a test case) and correctly identify which role produced it and which phase it belongs to
  • Explain what goes wrong on a project when a phase gets skipped or a role's artifact never gets produced
